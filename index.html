Solutions Engineering competency framework · IC2 to IC5 · Draft for review

How to use this framework

These competencies apply to every SE regardless of segment. What differs is the evidence, not the bar.

An SE in Velocity or Strat may demonstrate a competency through migration scoping, technical questions, product deep dives, or content that AEs deliver. An SE in Mid-Market may demonstrate the same competency through deep-dive demos and multi-stakeholder deal support. Neither path is the higher one, and neither is a prerequisite for the other. Volume of customer-facing demos is not a proxy for depth.

Each level shows only what is new at that level and assumes every level to its left. For the complete bar at one level, use One role in full.

For reviewers

Context on why the framework is shaped the way it is, including what was deliberately left out and what is still open.

Structure

Every competency level follows four beats: capability (what they can do), craft standard (the quality bar on how), scope (whose work beyond their own), and judgment and limits (what they escalate, and what they know not to attempt). The judgment beat is the one most competency frameworks omit, and it is where the real separation between levels lives.

Levels are capped at four sentences to keep the framework usable in a one-to-one conversation.

Deliberate exclusions

Results and metrics are not in the grid. No quota attainment, influenced MRR, or win rate. Competencies describe how someone works; results are tracked in performance reviews. Mixing them makes the framework read as a performance review and makes the "how" conversations harder to have honestly.

Personality traits are not in the grid. Earlier drafts included "can do attitude," "high energy," "emotional maturity," "resilience," "innovative, persuasive, creative," and "growth-oriented individual who self-regulates." These cannot be evidenced, get scored on communication style, and produce promotion decisions that are hard to defend in calibration and impossible to coach toward. Every trait was reattached to an observable situation instead.

"Can do attitude" was removed specifically because it penalizes the behaviour the senior levels require: telling an AE a timeline is unrealistic, telling a firm AI is the wrong answer, telling leadership what the team should not claim.

Deficit framing was removed. "Written communication may require review" and "may require oversight from senior members" describe what someone cannot do. Reframed as behaviour: IC2 seeks review, IC3 escalates when a situation exceeds their authority rather than their skill.

Solution Craft was drafted and removed. The craft of demonstrating the product varies too much by segment to grade in a single column, and the pending Demo Platform Engineer role would change who owns that work. Its two most valuable elements were redistributed: session recovery to Customer Engagement IC3, and validating a reusable asset works for someone who cannot ask a question to Systems and Process IC4.

The rule used for placing content: the artifact type does not decide the home, the subject does. A playbook about how the team operates goes in Systems and Process. A playbook about how the team performs in front of customers goes in Customer Engagement.

Open items

  • External recognition appears in two competencies (Platform Knowledge IC5 and AI Fluency IC5). Deliberate, since SEs are customer-facing, but the two are differentiated by audience and subject so one talk or article cannot satisfy both. Platform Knowledge is practice operations and platform authority aimed at firm leadership and industry press. AI Fluency is responsible AI aimed at risk, ethics, and firm security audiences.
  • The six buyer personas page is stale. Last modified March 2024, and the owning account is unlicensed. It lives in the Marketing space. IC2 depends on it, so it needs a refresh and an owner, or the competency should reference the concept rather than the artifact.
  • Quality and Rigour reads identically in both versions. Its levels never restated carried-forward behaviour, so there is nothing to strip. The comparison toggle will show no difference for that competency.
  • "Sustained track record" at IC5 sits slightly against the decision to keep results out of the grid. The alternative is to move it to a manager guide as a calibration principle.
  • A manager guide does not exist yet. Candidate contents: evidence examples per level, calibration principles such as sustained performance across multiple cycles rather than one strong quarter, and guidance on what counts as equivalent evidence across segments.
